B-rep solid models vs mesh models

A B-rep (boundary representation) solid describes a part as exact surfaces and edges that enclose a volume. A mesh approximates the surface with triangles. STEP and IGES files normally carry B-rep geometry. STL files carry a triangle mesh.

B-rep solidMesh
SurfaceExact surfaces and edgesFlat triangles that approximate the shape
Typical filesSTEP, IGESSTL, 3MF, GLB
Editing in CADFaces, edges and holes can be selected and changedHard to edit. Features are lost in the triangles
Machining and drawingsSuited to CNC programming and dimensioned drawingsPoorly suited. Curved faces are faceted
3D printing and viewingUsually converted to a mesh firstUsed directly by slicers and viewers

Which one do you need?

  • Send STEP when someone will machine the part, edit it, or put it in an assembly.
  • Send STL or 3MF when the next step is a 3D printer.
  • Use a mesh when you only need a quick look at the shape.

How NitoCAD uses both

The free preview is an STL mesh, so you can see the part before you commit. In CAD Studio, NitoCAD builds a true B-rep solid on OpenCASCADE and exports STEP and IGES for CAD and CAM work, DXF and PDF for drawings, and STL, 3MF and GLB when you need a mesh. See all export formats.

Converting a mesh back into an editable solid is a hard problem and rarely gives clean results. Starting from a B-rep solid avoids it.
